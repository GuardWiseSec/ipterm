Subnet mask cheat sheet: CIDR to netmask

Updated October 12, 2026 · 2 min read

CIDR notation writes a network as an address plus a prefix length: 192.168.1.0/24 means the first 24 bits identify the network and the remaining 8 identify hosts. This page converts every common prefix to its subnet mask.

Reading CIDR notation

  • Prefix length is the number of 1 bits in the subnet mask: /24 is 255.255.255.0 because the first 24 bits are 1.
  • Addresses in a block are 2(32 − prefix): a /24 has 256.
  • Usable hosts subtract the network address (the first) and the broadcast address (the last). Two exceptions: a /31 has two usable addresses for point-to-point links (RFC 3021), and a /32 is a single host.
  • Wildcard mask is the netmask inverted, used in router access lists and some firewall rules.
